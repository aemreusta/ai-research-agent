# Personal Data Breach Notification Comparison: GDPR vs. Türkiye's KVKK (as of September 2026)

## Summary

For notifying affected individuals, single-source sources report that GDPR requires communication without undue delay when there is a high risk to rights and freedoms, while KVKK mandates notification as soon as reasonably possible after affected persons are identified [k2, k6, k24, k25, k32]. _(single source)_ [1][2][3][4] Single-source findings indicate that GDPR provides specific notification exceptions for low-risk breaches, encrypted data, or neutralized risks, whereas KVKK allows phased submissions and limits breach announcements on the Authority's website to 60 days [k3, k23, k33, k43, k44]. _(single source)_ [1][3][4][5]

## Key Findings

- Single-source evidence indicates that data controllers must notify the supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach under both GDPR and KVKK Board Decision No. 2019/10 [k1, k13, k22, k35]. _(single source)_ [1][3][6][7]
- According to single-source findings, GDPR exempts controllers from notifying the supervisory authority if the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to natural persons' rights and freedoms [k23, k41]. _(single source)_ [3][5]
- Single-source findings report that GDPR mandates notifying affected individuals without undue delay only when a personal data breach poses a high risk to their rights and freedoms [k24, k25, k42]. _(single source)_ [3][5]
- Under KVKK, single-source sources establish that notification to affected persons must occur as soon as reasonably possible after identifying them, using direct notification or website publication [k2, k6, k7, k32]. _(single source)_ [1][2][4]
- According to single-source findings, GDPR provides exceptions from notifying individuals where the personal data was encrypted and made illegible, the risk has been neutralized, or direct notification would take disproportionate effort [k19, k43, k44, k45]. _(single source)_ [5][8]
- Single-source evidence shows that KVKK Board Decision No. 2019/271 defines minimum content elements for notifications to affected persons, and breach announcements on the Authority's website are limited to 60 days unless early removal is justified by proof of prior individual notice [k3, k4, k8, k14]. _(single source)_ [1][2][6]

## Conclusion

In summary, single-source evidence shows that GDPR and KVKK both maintain a 72-hour benchmark for supervisory authority notification [k1, k22, k35]. _(single source)_ [1][3][7] However, single-source findings indicate that they differ regarding individual notices, as GDPR applies a high-risk trigger while KVKK requires notification once affected persons are identified [k2, k6, k24, k32]. _(single source)_ [1][2][3][4] Additionally, single-source reports demonstrate that GDPR articulates specific statutory exceptions such as encryption and risk neutralization, whereas KVKK regulates procedural measures like 60-day website notices [k3, k43, k44]. _(single source)_ [1][5]
