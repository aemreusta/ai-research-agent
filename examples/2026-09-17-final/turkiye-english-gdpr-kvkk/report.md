# Comparison of Personal Data Breach Notification Rules: GDPR vs. KVKK

## Summary

For regulatory notifications, single sources report that both GDPR Article 33 and KVKK secondary procedures apply a 72-hour notification timeframe from awareness, although GDPR explicitly exempts breaches unlikely to result in a risk whereas KVKK Article 12/5 statutorily mandates notification as soon as possible when data is obtained unlawfully. _(single source)_ [1][2][3][4][5] When notifying affected individuals, single sources note that both regimes activate obligations when a breach poses a high risk to rights and freedoms, with GDPR requiring notice without undue delay and KVKK requiring notice as soon as possible. _(single source)_ [1][4][5][6][7][8] Single sources also indicate that both frameworks provide comparable exceptions to individual notification, including technical protections like encryption, subsequent risk mitigation, or disproportionate effort replaced by public communication. _(single source)_ [1][7][9]

## Key Findings

- Under Article 12/5 of Law No. 6698, a data controller must notify the Board and affected persons as soon as possible if processed personal data is obtained unlawfully, with breach response procedures established by Board Decision 2019/10 dated 24.01.2019, according to single sources. _(single source)_ [2][4][10][11]
- A single source reports that under GDPR Article 33, supervisory authorities must be notified within 72 hours of awareness unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms. _(single source)_ [1][3]
- For KVKK regulator notification, single sources report a requirement to notify the Board within 72 hours of learning of or detecting the breach, and controllers may request an extension within those 72 hours if more time is needed to determine the breach's scope. _(single source)_ [4][5][7]
- Under GDPR Article 34, communication to data subjects must occur without undue delay in clear and plain language if the breach is likely to result in a high risk to natural persons' rights and freedoms, as reported by single sources. _(single source)_ [1][6][8][9]
- For KVKK, single sources state that individual notification is required as soon as possible via email, SMS, post, or website announcement when the breach creates a high risk for data subjects' rights and freedoms. _(single source)_ [4][5][7]
- Both frameworks exempt controllers from directly notifying data subjects if the data was protected by technical measures like encryption, if subsequent actions ensure high risk is no longer likely, or if direct communication involves disproportionate effort requiring public announcements, according to single sources. _(single source)_ [1][7][8][9]
- Single sources report that supervisory authorities under both GDPR and KVKK have the authority to order controllers to inform individuals or to publicly announce the breach. _(single source)_ [2][3][4][8][9]

## Conclusion

Both GDPR and KVKK align on a 72-hour target for notifying authorities and set a high-risk trigger for notifying affected persons, with GDPR using 'without undue delay' and KVKK specifying 'as soon as possible', according to single sources. _(single source)_ [1][2][4] Exceptions to individual notice under both frameworks similarly center on strong technical protections, swift mitigation, or disproportionate effort paired with public notice, based on single-source findings. _(single source)_ [1][7][8][9]
